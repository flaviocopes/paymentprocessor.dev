### What we're doing

Create a standalone, evidence-led guide to choosing how to get paid online. Its first decision will be “Merchant of Record or remain the merchant yourself?”, followed by clearly typed options such as processor, wallet, acquirer/gateway, and billing layer so unlike services are never presented as equivalents. The site will own profiles, comparisons, education, and an effective-cost tool; `migratingto.dev` will remain the canonical home of detailed migration playbooks. The design will adapt the supplied Notion reference into a calm cobalt-and-off-white editorial system with thin keylines, strong tables, compact diagrams, monospaced evidence labels, and visible review dates.

### Steps

1. [x] Establish the product boundary and working identity. Use `paymentprocessor.dev` as the practical domain but brand the publication more accurately as “Payment Stack Guide,” with “Merchant of Record or direct payments?” as its central question; broader domain directions are `paymentstack.guide`, `payments.guide`, and `getpaidstack.com`, with availability unconfirmed.
2. [x] Create an evidence ledger and two launch catalogs. The MoR catalog will publish Paddle, Polar, Gumroad, FastSpring, Stripe Managed Payments, and Lemon Squeezy, with Creem and Dodo Payments held as research candidates. The “you remain the merchant” catalog will publish Stripe Payments, PayPal Checkout, Braintree, Adyen, Checkout.com, Mollie, Square, and Authorize.net; this is a representative importance-and-use-case set, not a popularity ranking.
3. [x] Define Astro Content Collection schemas for `providers`, `comparisons`, `guides`, `sources`, and `migrationLinks`. Each provider will declare `legalSeller`, `serviceModel`, seller and buyer geography, target business size, product eligibility, payment methods, checkout, subscriptions, invoicing, tax calculation versus filing/remittance, fraud, disputes, customer support, APIs, payouts, portability, and composable fee rules. Record claim-level URLs, review dates, public versus negotiated pricing, country/preview/eligibility flags, contradictions, and unknowns; validate missing or stale evidence and prevent a billing tool or tax add-on from being mislabeled as a processor or MoR.
4. [x] Ship a focused MVP: home decision path; `/merchant-of-record/` and `/payment-processors/` hubs; fourteen launch profiles; four scenario-written comparisons (Paddle vs Polar, Paddle vs Stripe Payments, Stripe Payments vs Stripe Managed Payments, and Stripe Payments vs PayPal Checkout); three guides (MoR versus processor, what you still handle when you remain the merchant, and migration effects on subscriptions); `/migrations/`; `/tools/effective-cost/`; and `/methodology/`. Filters will start with seller country, B2C/B2B, subscriptions, average transaction value, international mix, and required payment methods, without generating every possible pair.
5. [x] Make comparisons and the calculator responsibility-aware. Show MoR advantages such as outsourced indirect-tax filing and transaction support alongside tradeoffs such as higher fees, product restrictions, buyer-facing merchant identity, payout timing, and provider dependence; show direct-payment control and potentially lower processing cost alongside the seller's continuing tax, dispute, support, billing, and compliance work. Calculate documented payment fees from revenue, transaction count, subscriptions, international-card share, FX, refunds, disputes, and plan fees; show formulas and confidence labels, and accept user-entered operating costs rather than inventing tax, accounting, or staffing estimates.
6. [x] Keep each site self-canonical. The guide site will write unique migration summaries and link to the exact `migratingto.dev/guides/{source}-to-{destination}` execution playbook; `migratingto.dev` will link back to provider and comparison context. Do not duplicate playbook bodies or point canonicals at merely related pages. Treat the intentionally retired `merchant-of-record.astro` comparison as research input only if it is recovered, and do not restore it on `migratingto.dev`.
7. [x] Build with Astro, accessible semantic HTML, minimal Alpine.js/HTMX, and Cloudflare Pages compatibility, then add sitemap and structured data, claim/source/freshness validation, responsive comparison tables, keyboard and contrast checks, and a scheduled editorial review report. Use the current six-platform migration matrix and playbook schema as the integration source of truth.

### What we're NOT doing

- No domain purchase, production DNS, or production deployment without approval.
- No copied vendor language, unexplained scores, fake ratings, legal/tax/accounting advice, or affiliate-first recommendations.
- No claim that “non-MoR” is one product category, no popularity ranking without defensible usage evidence, and no claim that a tax or billing add-on transfers merchant responsibility.
- No publication of every possible pair, Creem, or Dodo Payments before the evidence and editorial checks pass.
- No duplicate migration execution guides on the new site and no rewrite of unrelated `migratingto.dev` playbooks.
- No exact calculator output for custom, negotiated, undocumented, or disputed fees.

### How we'll know it works

- Every material provider and comparison claim resolves to a dated first-party source or is visibly marked unknown, restricted, preview, negotiated, or contradictory; the legal seller and retained responsibilities are always explicit.
- A reader can choose between an MoR and remaining the merchant, narrow fourteen launch providers by actual business constraints, inspect a reproducible fee estimate, and reach the correct canonical migration playbook.
- The MVP builds for Cloudflare Pages and passes responsive, keyboard, contrast, canonical, sitemap, structured-data, and internal-link checks while visibly matching the supplied cobalt editorial design language.
